Process & Exploration

1. week5.1

I modified the repeated arcs from the previous class, making each unit rotate around its center point. At first, I used 360 degrees for the rotation, but I forgot that the shape is symmetrical, so the plotter ended up drawing the same paths twice. Later, based on the repeated arc pattern, I added random vertical parallel lines to make the overall pattern more visually interesting.

2. week5.2

In this version, I tried to further develop the pattern from the previous class. I added more arcs to each original arc by changing their width and height to make the pattern more visually complex. I also added a `sin()` value to transform the overall pattern, creating a wave-like effect across the whole composition. However, because I did not set boundaries for the drawing, some parts extended outside the canvas on the webpage. I scaled down the whole pattern in Inkscape and finally drew it successfully with the plotter.

3. week5.3 clock

My idea is to represent time through falling water drops and the changing water level. Every second, a white water drop falls from the top of the screen. At the same time, the background gradually becomes lighter, so the white drop slowly blends into the background. After 60 seconds, the water level rises a little, representing one minute. Then a new minute begins, and the background changes to another random color. It gradually becomes lighter again. This process keeps repeating, and the water level rises every minute. After one hour, the water fills the entire canvas. At the end of the hour, the water level gradually goes down until the canvas is empty, and the clock starts a new cycle. As the water drop falls, I gradually increase the change in its y-position each frame to simulate gravity. This makes the drop fall faster and faster, creating a more natural movement.

3. week5.3 clock2

I felt that the previous version of my clock looked a bit too simple, so I wanted to add a gradient effect. I used the map() function to change the lightness in HSL colors, creating a gradient by layering short horizontal lines with different lightness values. I also used the sin() function to calculate the wave height, giving the gradient a wavy shape.
